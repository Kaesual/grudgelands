# Round 36 — Story bible v2: The Undertithe

Revision of the first draft (Astra, v1; kept outside the repository) after an
independent review, worked out with the user on 2026-10-05. It keeps v1's
outline and fixes its logic, its names and its stale beats. Bound by
[Round 36 §2.1–2.9 and §4.1](../round36-plan.md)
and the coordinator's fixed points: levels 41 / 46 / 53 / 60; one shared
rift at **Tombroad Ambush**; the commanders in the Gravesalt and Skyglass
war camps; chapter 2's solo captain in a war camp on a 41–50 battlefield
(variant A); no Nether content; the factions are only The Accord and
The Throng.

"Tone" means the wording of quest texts only. The game has no voice
recordings and gets none.

## 1. The threat

**The Undertithe** is an ancient hunger beneath the world. It counts the
dead as payment owed to it and reaches into the overworld as fresh,
devouring heat. Heat and malice seep through flaws in the earth the way
furnace heat passes through masonry. The **Tithe-Brand**, burned through
the faces of ordinary battlefield coins, draws that influence along the
path the coin travels. No portal opens; the money does the work.

### 1.1 The rule of the branded coin

Branded coin binds whoever takes it as pay, living or dead.

- A **living** holder feels the coin stay warm. It cannot be spent, lost
  or thrown away; it cools only when it is passed on as pay for a death.
  The holder's own name is already on the roll.
- A **dead** soldier paid in branded coin cannot rest while the debt
  stands. Paying the dead with it raises them; renewing the pay keeps them
  on duty.
- Every name paid in branded coin is entered on the Undertithe's **roll**.
  The Undertithe counts that roll as a deed of ownership: the names on it
  belong to it.

This is the line between the corruption and the Undead people. The blight
of Nhal Veyr is old death, and the Undead who live under it are free.
Branded dead are *owned*. Being Undead is neither evidence of guilt nor
protection against the brand.

### 1.2 The circuit

The brand spreads by changing hands, so the scheme is a loop:

1. **The soot factors** strip coin from the dead of both armies and burn
   the brand into it.
2. **Paymaster Chirr** pays his Last-Pay Archers with it; each archer's
   name goes on the roll.
3. **Toll-Taker Senn** pays his raised dead with it and collects tolls on a
   road kept deadly on purpose; every traveller killed there is a name.
4. The tolls flow back to the factors, are branded again and go out once
   more. Every death along the loop is tithed below.

Ninepins and Huskell sit on the same loop: Ninepins pledges his standard
against the next payment, and Huskell's watch is re-enlisted with fresh
pay so its names never leave the roll.

### 1.3 The soot factors

They call themselves **soot factors**: agents who handle the soot-black
pay. Each of them took the first warm coin out of greed and now cannot
stop. A factor who refuses to pass the coin on with a death attached burns
from the purse inward. Their rank and file are the front's existing
brigands and the **Saltroad Deserters**, who strip the tomb galleries of
Gravesalt Escarpment and call it salvage. Their instructions arrive
scorched into account sheets: keep the roads dangerous, keep soldiers
dying, press the brand into every place where wages change hands. Nobody
among them has met the hand that writes the sheets.

### 1.4 Earliest traces (levels 11–30, no edits needed)

Both factions' early zones already tell of crooked ledgers and dead who
owe debts. In this story they are the Undertithe's apprenticeship: small
collectors with *unbranded* stamps, before the fire learned to use the
coin. Later givers may recognise them; no quest requires the player to
remember them.

- **The Accord:** the counting-house stamp on Nog's purses (Copperfell),
  the forged bough-seal and Bough-Counter Rusk (Starbough Vale, Lethariel),
  Quartermaster Scrip selling the line's supper under the Accord's own
  supply seal (Ashenward March, 33).
- **The Throng:** Mortuary Clerk Hush entering the Throng dead as debtors,
  the Tithe Revenants and Debtbound Zombies (Nhal Veyr), the Reed-Counter's
  tally (Mournfen), Bandit Tithe-Keeper Vek's rain tithe (Raincall Basin).

### 1.5 The body at the rift

At Tombroad Ambush, where the roll's unpaid balance gathers, the will
behind the payments takes an overworld body of slag and battlefield ash:
**Isquarre the Tithe-Eater**. The generations-old war was never its
invention; the profiteers made that war its feeding ground. Gravesalt's
night **Rift Spawn**, long familiar to the escarpment, are the first
things to answer the brand there.

## 2. The faction lines

Each spine runs through its fortress Warmaster's free second line and
existing givers. Chapters open at **41, 46 and 53**, each requiring the
previous chapter's last turn-in; the finale opens at **60**. The named
front climaxes are folded in; unrelated front quests stay ungated.
Optional encounters never block progress or hold essential clues.

The traitors (§2, chapter 2) are **text-only figures**: named in orders,
ledgers and dialogue, never placed as NPCs or mobs.

### The Accord — Every Name Accounted For

**Tone.** The Warmaster at **Ashenward Bastion**: restrained, exact,
impatient with glorious reports that leave out the casualties. Evidence
must survive scrutiny; orders do not absolve whoever signed them. The
Accord's weakness is its love of control: it keeps its own shame under
seal. Anchor line: "Bring me the account. I already have enough
accusations."

#### Chapter 1 — Ash on the Causeway (41–46)

Through Cinderline Watch and Alna Archsight at Archshadow Post, the player
follows branded pay from Causeway Brigands to the dead collecting Senn's
tolls. Archsight's runesmiths know the style of the stamp from the old
Copperfell counting-house forgeries, but this one is burned, not inked.
**Collection Ends Here** (`archshadow_front_03`) brings Senn down. His
accounts show that he paid his dead in branded coin and was paid to keep
the road deadly, not merely to charge for it. They also carry a pay bundle
wrapped in a requisition attributed to The Throng, and a note of later
payments from Paymaster Chirr, who is still out of reach in The Skyglass
Canopy. The requisition paper is good, the kind a fortress uses. The
Warmaster suspects a Throng pay scheme but refuses to count an Undead body
as proof of who financed it. **War Construct Last-Toll** stays the
optional **Group: No More Toll** branch.

#### Chapter 2 — Orders Behind the Lines (46–52)

**Nobody Left to Rally** (`lasthedge_front_04`) brings down Standard-Bearer
Ninepins, whose renewed pay keeps dead soldiers rallying around a war
already lost. The fortress's camp raids lead behind enemy lines to **The
Throng's high war camp on The Shattered Line**. The mandatory solo step is
to kill its captain and take the captain's orders.

The orders say two things. First, The Throng is hunting the same factors:
it lists seized branded pay and its own soldiers burned by it. Second, the
seized pay was wrapped in **genuine Ashenward requisition forms**,
countersigned by **Requisition Clerk Odren Vell**, who kept the books for
the Causeway convoys after Quartermaster Scrip's fall and left the Bastion
last season "to settle arrears". The forged Throng requisition from
chapter 1 was written on the Accord's own paper.

The Warmaster revises the accusation, orders a quiet audit and seals the
finding. The Throng must not learn that Accord paper fed this. The player
is sent to reach the payers first; a rival investigation is no invitation
to share intelligence. **War Construct Engine Nine** stays an optional
**Group:** climax; it still grinds forward on orders somebody keeps
paying for. The orders also name **War Commander Stonegrudge**, now
posted to The Throng's war camp in Gravesalt Escarpment. Hunting the
commander is an optional **Group:** branch at that camp's level (see fit
notes), and the existing raid on that camp warns that the commander now
fights with its garrison.

#### Chapter 3 — What the Accounts Conceal (53–60)

Borin Splitbolt's **The Last Watch Ends** (`splitbolt_front_08`) releases
Watch-Captain Huskell's soldiers. Huskell's roll is not a muster list but
a page of the Undertithe's roll: soldiers of both armies, re-enlisted with
fresh branded pay. At the bottom, in new ink, is **Odren Vell**. The coin
collected its own clerk.

The trail runs through Eriath Boughwarden to **The Price of Another War**
(`lethariel_front_04`). Eriath knows Rusk's reckoning, and Chirr's payroll
is the same hand grown bold: names of both banners, each one bought.
Chirr's purse holds coin from the dead of both armies. The player strikes
the names from the roll, at the burning roots of The Skyglass Canopy and at
the tally-stone of Tombroad Ambush. Every struck name leaves the debt
unsecured, and an unsecured debt is collected in person: the remaining
balance gathers at Tombroad Ambush. The solo chapter ends with that report
to the Warmaster.

The Warmaster enters Vell's name in the Bastion's own roll anyway, with
the cause beside it: "Every name accounted for. His too." **Bog Witch
Salt-Counter**, who counts soldiers as expendable entries, and **Serpent
Glass-Throat**, fattened amid the corruption (folding in **Beneath the
Waiting Coils**), stay optional **Group:** hunts. The Accord now
understands the common threat and still means to claim the victory before
The Throng can claim authority over the site.

### The Throng — Our Oaths Are Ours

**Tone.** The Warmaster at **Bannerbreak Warhold**: blunt, proud of useful
courage, contemptuous of bought loyalty. Orc resolve, Troll memory and the
Undead's dead belong to nobody's paymaster. The Throng's weakness is its
pride: it says its shame out loud and calls that honour. Anchor line: "A
bought oath is a debt with a blade on it. Ours were never for sale."

#### Chapter 1 — No Coin for Our Dead (41–46)

Drek Rampbinder's Causeway work turns up pay bundles sealed as Accord
requisitions, with fresh burns across coins stripped from old graves.
**Payment in Full** (`bb_front_ramp_04`) kills Toll-Taker Senn and shows
that he paid his dead in branded coin to keep corpses on the road, not
merely to collect fares. A remittance names Paymaster Chirr, still in The
Skyglass Canopy, and promises another payment for every crossing made
unsafe. The Warmaster takes the forged seal as proof that The Accord is
buying a cheaper army. Orrel Hollowstep sets the new scorch apart from the
old death he knows; the way the coins are entered reminds him of Mortuary
Clerk Hush's ledger, except that Hush never had fire. **War Construct
Last-Toll** stays Orrel's optional **Group: Close the Account** branch;
nobody must fight an elite to learn who paid Senn.

#### Chapter 2 — Whose Orders? (46–52)

Yarra Standardmender's **Let That Banner Fall** (`bb_front_torn_05`)
defeats Standard-Bearer Ninepins and shows a standard pledged again and
again against the next payment. Fortress raids carry the hunt behind enemy
lines to **The Accord's high war camp on The Shattered Line**, where the
mandatory solo target is its captain, carrying orders.

The orders list confiscated pay and Accord soldiers burned by it, and
command a hunt for the soot factors: The Accord has been robbed and burned
too. Worse, the seized coin is listed by its **Warhold war-chest tally
marks**: coin from Throng graves, entered by **Tallykeeper Mardra** of
Bannerbreak Warhold, who left last winter "to collect what the dead were
owed". The Warhold's own tallykeeper sold its dead.

The Warmaster believes the evidence, strikes Mardra from the Warhold's
roll in front of the drill yard, and refuses to give up the pursuit, lest
The Accord impound the truth along with the coin. **War Construct Engine
Nine** stays an optional **Group:** climax; it still grinds forward on
orders somebody keeps paying for. The orders name **War
Commander Greyvow**, now posted to The Accord's war camp in The Skyglass
Canopy; hunting the commander is an optional **Group:** branch at that
camp's level, and the existing raid on that camp warns of the commander.

#### Chapter 3 — The Dead Owe Nothing (53–60)

Vaska Ashlistener's **Dismissed, Captain** (`bw_front_ashveil_04`) ends
Watch-Captain Huskell's roll call. Vaska hears what the roll is: names
bought from both armies and kept on duty with fresh pay. The newest name
is **Mardra**. Nalo Pathdrum's **Promises with Bowstrings** (`kz_front_04`)
brings down Paymaster Chirr, whose promises to his Last-Pay Archers hide
branded pay for burned marks rather than wages for holding land. Vaska
reads the insult to the dead, Nalo the damage to living roots. The player
burns the bought names off the roll at the canopy roots and at the
tally-stone of Tombroad Ambush. With its names gone, the debt has nothing
left to hold, and the collector comes for it at Tombroad Ambush. The
player reports this to the Warmaster to finish the solo chapter.

The Warmaster will not let the Undertithe have even Mardra: "That debt is
ours to judge, not his." **Bog Witch Salt-Counter** and **Serpent
Glass-Throat** keep **Group: Nobody's Number** and **Group: More Than One
Voice** as optional branches. The Throng will break the thing that claims
its dead and carry its own banner home, whether The Accord likes the story
or not.

### Shared finale — Group: The Collector Comes Due

At level 60, two or three players confront **Isquarre the Tithe-Eater** at
**Tombroad Ambush**, under their own faction's commission. There is no
truce and no joint turn-in. Isquarre treats both armies as unpaid
accounts and their dead as his property. Defeat breaks his body, but the
shallow, harmful crack remains, and the Tithe-Eater can come back to
collect again (the respawn). Each faction reports to its own Warmaster and
gets its own last line (§5).

### Fit notes

- **Chapter 1 vs. Senn's level:** Senn's shipped climaxes open at 48
  (`archshadow_front_03`) and 46 (`bb_front_ramp_04`), while chapter 2
  opens at 46 and requires chapter 1's last turn-in. This bible does not
  change levels; the Q lanes settle it.
- **Commanders after variant A:** chapter 2's mandatory captain sits on
  The Shattered Line (existing high-camp raids, 47–49). The commanders
  stay in the Gravesalt and Skyglass war camps (raids there open at 57).
  Chapter 2's orders introduce them; their optional **Group:** quests
  therefore sit at those camps' level, in practice during chapter 3.
- **Chirr appears twice per faction:** his archers already work the
  Saltroad Cliffs (`splitbolt_front_05`, "Their paymaster got his money's
  worth"). Texts may use that as his shadow in chapters 1–2 without a
  meeting.

## 5. The finale's last line

Each faction's final Warmaster turn-in ends with its own line. Both point
**below** and imply that something there acts; neither promises travel
(V2).

- **The Accord:** "We closed his account. Beneath us, someone turned a
  fresh page."
- **The Throng:** "His last blow struck the ground. The answering blow
  came from underneath."
