# Armor format (Round 54)

The contract between the armor art, the model generator, the bake and the game, from Round 54
([plan](../planning/round54-plan.md), background in the [armor redesign plan](../planning/armor-redesign-plan.md)).
The files live in [`tools/r54_armor/`](../../tools/r54_armor/README.md); `catalog.py`, `data/catalog.json` and
`data/atlas.json` change only through the round's coordinator.

## 1. Shapes

Armor is a set of low-poly boxes baked into the character model, one catalog for every armored character. A box
that a piece does not use is hidden by texture alpha (0), so the model never changes with the gear.

- **Units:** skin pixels. One pixel is 0.525 model units (0.0525 nodes; the 32 px character is 1.68 nodes tall).
  Rest pose, y up from the soles, +z forward (the face), the character's right is −x (right-handed, as the
  render tool and three.js). The `.b3d` mesh space is left-handed, with the character's right at **+x**: a catalog
  point (x, y, z) is the model point 0.525 × (−x, y, z), and that x flip reverses the triangle winding (so does
  the mirrored copy's).
- **Catalog:** 17 shapes, 28 boxes with the mirrored copies, 864 vertices with the body and the cloak (24 per
  box). Budget for the player model: 45 boxes. `python3 tools/r54_armor/zfight.py` checks that no two boxes (body
  included, all bones, rest pose) have same-facing faces closer than 0.25 px.
- **Bones** (pivots from `character.b3d`): body (0, 12, 0), head (0, 24, 0), arm_r (−6, 22, 0), leg_r (−2, 12, 0),
  and two swing bones hinged at the belt: panel_f (0, 12.75, 3.5) and panel_b (0, 12.75, −3.5). The panels swing
  with the legs: front 0.06 rad + 1.2 × |leg angle|, back 0.06 rad + 1.1 × |leg angle|; the cloak never swings less
  than the back panel.
- **Mirrored boxes** (`mirror: true`) get a left copy mirrored in x on arm_l, leg_l or the body, with the **same
  UVs**, so the left copy shows the sheet mirrored, as `character.b3d` does for the left arm and leg. (The mirrored
  copy's triangle winding is flipped so it still faces outwards.)
- **Rotated boxes** (`rot: [axis, degrees, origin]`): the box is built in its own axes and turned about `origin`;
  its sheet uses the unrotated sizes.

## 2. Texture slots of a character

In this order in the model's texture list:

| Slot | Size | Content |
|---|---|---|
| `skin` | 64 × 32 | the race look plus the inner armor paint (§5), as before Round 54 |
| `cloak` | 32 × 32 | the cloak, as before |
| `armor` | 128 × 64 | the armor atlas (§3) |
| `weapon` | per weapon | the weapon card in the hand (inventory view; transparent in the world) |

An empty slot (no armor at all, no weapon shown) is `blank.png`, like the cloak today. The player model carries all
four slots. Armored NPCs (a visual spec with an `armor_line`: guards and bandits today) use the generated **NPC
model**: the same body and armor buffer with `skin`, `armor`, `weapon` (no cloak), built from a list of the boxes
NPCs wear; that list is **every catalog box** for now (NPCs wear every tier). Unarmored humanoid NPCs (kings, royal
guards, vendors, villagers) keep `character.b3d`.

The helmet is a box: when a head piece is worn, the race look drops the hair layer as before (the render tool
draws the bald look then), and the face stays the race's.

## 3. The armor atlas

`data/atlas.json` (written by `atlas.py`, checked by `atlas.py --check`) places every shape's box sheet once in a
**128 × 64** atlas: shelves left to right, sorted by sheet height, then width (descending), then id; no gutter; rows
0–41 used, the rest free for later boxes. Per box: `rect` = [x, y, w, h] of its sheet and `faces` = its six face
rectangles in atlas texels, origin top left; a UV is u = x / 128, v = y / 64. The model's UVs, the baked layers and
the game's composition all use this one layout.

A baked atlas layer is the full 128 × 64 with only one slot's boxes painted (§7); a character's armor texture is its
four slot layers overlaid, like the skin layers.

## 4. Box sheets

Each box has one sheet in the classic skin unwrap. Texel count per edge: `floor(size + 0.5)`, at least 1, from the
box's size in skin pixels (`w` = x, `h` = y, `d` = z; rotated boxes in their own axes). The sheet is `2d + 2w` wide
and `d + h` high, origin top left:

| Face | Position | Size |
|---|---|---|
| top | (d, 0) | w × d (its top row is the back edge) |
| bottom | (d + w, 0) | w × d (its top row is the front edge) |
| right (the character's right, −x) | (0, d) | d × h |
| front (+z) | (d, d) | w × h |
| left (+x) | (d + w, d) | d × h |
| back (−z) | (2d + w, d) | w × h |

Side faces: row 0 is the top. Front: column 0 is the character's right. Back: column 0 is the character's left.
Anything outside the six rectangles stays transparent. Faces are independent: transparency in a side face does not
remove the top or bottom face.

**Armor is opaque** (the user, 2026-10-20: transparency showed holes, since one looks into the hollow box): every
texel of every face of a box sheet is opaque. The only exception is the helm's **face window**: the texels of its
front face that lie wholly in front of the head's face (columns 1–8, rows 1–8 of the 10 × 10 front face) may stay
transparent, so a helmet can leave the face open; the head is directly behind them. Shape comes from the box and
the paint, not from cut-outs.

## 5. Inner paint (on the skin)

Per slot, a 64 × 32 image in the classic skin layout, painted only in the slot's own regions (fixed, so the draw
order of the slots does not matter):

| Slot | Regions (x0, y0)–(x1, y1), exclusive ends |
|---|---|
| chest | torso (16, 16)–(40, 32), arm (40, 16)–(56, 32) |
| legs | leg (0, 20)–(16, 28): the top of the leg to mid-calf |
| feet | leg (0, 28)–(16, 32): the lowest four rows |
| head | none (the helmet is a box; the face stays the race's) |

## 6. Painted art

The painter's files, in one art directory:

| File | What | Size |
|---|---|---|
| `<line>/<box>_<variant>.png` | a box sheet | §4 |
| `<line>/inner/<slot>_<variant>.png` | inner paint, slot chest, legs or feet | 64 × 32 |
| `<line>/icons/<slot>_<variant>.png` | inventory icon, slot head, chest, legs or feet | 16 × 16 |
| `tiers.json` | which boxes, variants and palette each tier uses | |
| `palettes.json` (optional) | proposed tier palettes, laid over `data/palettes.json` by name | |

`<line>` is `metal`, `leather` or `cloth`; `<variant>` a short id (`a`, `b`, …). One sheet per box, line and
variant serves every tier that names it; mirrored boxes have one sheet.

**Reference palette.** Paint only with these 13 colours plus full transparency; alpha is 0 or 255, nothing between.

| Index | Name | RGB | Meaning |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | M_HI | `#F0F0F0` | material highlight |
| 2 | M_LIGHT | `#C8C8C8` | material light |
| 3 | M_MID | `#A0A0A0` | material base |
| 4 | M_DARK | `#787878` | material shade |
| 5 | M_DEEP | `#505050` | material deep shade, seams |
| 6 | OUTLINE | `#282828` | gaps, slits, darkest line |
| 7 | ACC_LIGHT | `#A07850` | accent material (straps, under-leather, rope), light |
| 8 | ACC_DARK | `#6E5034` | accent material, dark |
| 9 | A_LIGHT | `#E07070` | zone A (prefix): cloth, tabard, lining, stole, light |
| 10 | A_DARK | `#B04848` | zone A, dark |
| 11 | B_LIGHT | `#70A0E0` | zone B (suffix): fittings, buckles, rivets, hem, gem, light |
| 12 | B_DARK | `#4870B0` | zone B, dark |
| 13 | GLOW | `#FFC040` | glow (ember seams); M_LIGHT on palettes without glow |

**Tier palettes** give each name a colour per tier (`data/palettes.json`, 18 palettes, all 13 names as `#RRGGBB`).
Zone A and B carry neutral colours there (undyed linen, bare metal or brass).

**Zones.** The prefix colours the cloth (zone A, indices 9–10), the suffix the fittings (zone B, 11–12). Every slot
of every tier shows, from the front, at least 6 zone A and 3 zone B pixels (box sheets and inner paint together;
only the body and the same slot's boxes count as hiding them), so one enchanted piece shows its colour.

**Icons.** 16 × 16, reference palette, palette-swapped per tier. An icon shows recognisably the same intended
piece, not a copy of the 3D shape: one clear silhouette, readable at 32 and 16 px, few details.

**`tiers.json`.** Every box may be used on every tier of every line; the painter chooses by the design goals.
A missing variant entry means `a`; a tier entry must name its `palette`, and one without `inner` or `icon` has none.

```json
{"metal": {"T1": {"palette": "bronze", "on": ["helm", "chest", "thigh", "boot"],
                  "variant": {"helm": "a", "chest": "a"}, "inner": {"chest": "a", "legs": "a", "feet": "a"},
                  "icon": {"head": "a", "chest": "a", "legs": "a", "feet": "a"}}}}
```

Tier `T<n>` of a line is the n-th grade of that line in `grug_gear.MATERIALS` (metal bronze … abyssal_steel,
leather light … nightscale, cloth patch … stormweave).

## 7. Baked game files

Written by the bake from the art, the palettes, `tiers.json` and the atlas; read by `grug_visuals`. `<grade>` is
the game's grade key (`grug_gear.MATERIALS`; leather T4 keeps the key `scaled` under the display name Hardened).
The atlas and skin layers and their masks go to `mods/PLAYER/grug_visuals/textures/`, the icons stay in
`mods/ITEMS/grug_gear/textures/`. Every line, grade and slot gets its files, empty (transparent) where the tier
paints nothing: 72 atlas layers (head, chest, legs, feet) and 54 skin layers (chest, legs, feet; the head has no
skin region), each with its mask.

| File | Content |
|---|---|
| `grug_armor_<line>_<grade>_<slot>.png` | atlas layer, 128 × 64, only this slot's boxes painted |
| `grug_armor_<line>_<grade>_<slot>_skin.png` | skin layer, 64 × 32, the slot's inner paint |
| `…_ench.png` next to each | the enchant mask in today's format (grey + alpha, two frames stacked: frame 0 zone A for the prefix colour, frame 1 zone B for the suffix colour, grey from the art's shading), derived from the zone pixels, never drawn separately |
| `grug_gear_item_<slot>_<line>_<grade>.png` | the item icon (+ its mask), names unchanged |

## 8. Checks

From `tools/r54_armor/`:

- `python3 check.py --art <dir>`: file names and sizes, colours, alpha, transparent texels in a face (outside the
  helm's face window), paint outside faces or regions, missing files per tier, zone counts from the front, the
  overlap report; exit 1 on a violation.
- `python3 render.py --art <dir> --out <dir>`: contact sheets per line and race (`--detail` for one tier,
  `--engine soft` by default, sandbox-safe).
- `python3 export.py --check`, `python3 atlas.py --check`, `python3 zfight.py`: the contract data is current and
  the geometry clean.
- `test_art/` passes `check.py` without warnings; `test_art_broken/` exercises the violations.
