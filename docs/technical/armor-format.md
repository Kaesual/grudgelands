# Armor format (Round 54)

The contract between the armor art, the model generator, the bake and the game, from Round 54
([plan](../planning/round54-plan.md), background in the [armor redesign plan](../planning/armor-redesign-plan.md)).
The files live in [`tools/r54_armor/`](../../tools/r54_armor/README.md); `catalog.py`, `data/catalog.json` and
`data/atlas.json` change only through the round's coordinator.

## 1. Shapes

Armor is a set of low-poly boxes, and since Round 56 polygon parts (§1a), baked into the character model, one
catalog for every armored character. A box or part that a piece does not use is hidden by texture alpha (0), so the
model never changes with the gear.

- **Units:** skin pixels. One pixel is 0.525 model units (0.0525 nodes; the 32 px character is 1.68 nodes tall).
  Rest pose, y up from the soles, +z forward (the face), the character's right is −x (right-handed, as the
  render tool and three.js). The `.b3d` mesh space is left-handed, with the character's right at **+x**: a catalog
  point (x, y, z) is the model point 0.525 × (−x, y, z), and that x flip reverses the triangle winding (so does
  the mirrored copy's).
- **Catalog:** 17 shapes, 28 boxes with the mirrored copies, and one polygon part, the hood (8 faces, 32 vertices,
  16 triangles): 896 vertices with the body and the cloak (24 per box; 864 before the hood). The armor buffer of
  either model: 704 vertices, 352 triangles (672 and 336 before). Budget for the player model: 45 boxes.
  `python3 tools/r54_armor/zfight.py` checks that no two boxes (body included, all bones, rest pose) have
  same-facing faces closer than 0.25 px, nor a polygon part's face and a box's; the model generator checks every
  triangle of the built models.
- **Bones** (pivots from `character.b3d`): body (0, 12, 0), head (0, 24, 0), arm_r (−6, 22, 0), leg_r (−2, 12, 0),
  and two swing bones hinged at the belt: panel_f (0, 12.75, 3.5) and panel_b (0, 12.75, −3.5). The panels swing
  with the legs: front 0.06 rad + 1.2 × |leg angle|, back 0.06 rad + 1.1 × |leg angle|; the cloak never swings less
  than the back panel.
- **Mirrored boxes** (`mirror: true`) get a left copy mirrored in x on arm_l, leg_l or the body, with the **same
  UVs**, so the left copy shows the sheet mirrored, as `character.b3d` does for the left arm and leg. (The mirrored
  copy's triangle winding is flipped so it still faces outwards.)
- **Rotated boxes** (`rot: [axis, degrees, origin]`): the box is built in its own axes and turned about `origin`;
  its sheet uses the unrotated sizes.

### 1a. Polygon parts (Round 56)

"Boxes only" was a tool convention; the goal is low poly, and convex polygon faces (triangles, wedges) are allowed
where they save polygons or shape better (Round 56 ruling 14). A **polygon part** is a set of convex planar faces
on one bone, in the same frame as the boxes: skin pixels, rest pose, **absolute catalog coordinates** (not relative
to the bone's pivot). The first one is the **hood** (head slot, head bone; rulings 13–18), from the hood workshop
page, which exports exactly this format. The user accepted the page's first shape unchanged (2026-10-22), so
[`tools/r56_hood/hood.json`](../../tools/r56_hood/hood.json) holds the final values.

```json
{"format": "grudgelands-armor-poly 1", "id": "hood", "slot": "head", "bone": "head",
 "params": {}, "offsets": {},
 "vertices": [[x, y, z], ...],
 "sheet": [w, h],
 "faces": [{"name": "T.top", "piece": "T", "v": [0, 1, 2, 3], "uv": [[u, v], ...], "rect": [x, y, w, h]}]}
```

- `vertices`: catalog points in skin pixels (rounded to 4 decimals). The part is symmetric about x = 0.
- `faces`: each a convex planar polygon (planar within 0.001 px), its vertex indices **counter-clockwise seen from
  outside** in the right-handed catalog frame (the model's x flip reverses the winding, as for boxes, §1). The
  generator fans each face into triangles from its first vertex, with flat per-face normals (vertices duplicated
  per face, as on the boxes).
- **A closed shell:** the part and the helm shell together are closed (every edge of a hood face is shared by two
  hood faces or lies on the helm's surface); faces inside the shell (on the helm's surface, or shared by two pieces)
  are not emitted, and no inner or two-sided faces are needed. Coplanar faces of two pieces that share an edge are
  one face when their union is convex (one rectangle, no paint seam).
- **Unwrap:** `uv` per vertex in sheet texels (origin top left, as the box sheets). Seen from outside, u runs to
  the viewer's right and v down; "up" is world +y projected into the face, and for a near-horizontal face
  (|normal y| > 0.954, within about 17.5° of horizontal) the back edge (a top face) or the front edge (a bottom
  face) is the top row, as on the box sheets. A face's texel size is `floor(extent + 0.5)` per axis, at least 1.
  `rect` is the face's rectangle on the sheet; rectangles do not overlap.
- **Paint:** a face owns its whole `rect`; every texel of it is opaque when the part is worn (the texels outside the
  polygon but inside the rect are bleed, painted like their neighbours, so a diagonal edge never samples a
  transparent texel). Armor stays opaque (§4): a tier either paints the whole part or leaves it wholly transparent
  (the part not in its `on` list). Metal leaves the hood transparent (ruling 15). **A tier that names `hood` in its
  `on` list names `helm` too** (the hood has no faces inside the shell, so without the helm it would be hollow);
  `check.py` enforces it. The painted sheet is `<line>/hood_<variant>.png` at the `sheet` size (§6).
- **Atlas:** box sheets keep their shelf packing and their places byte-identical; polygon part sheets are packed
  after them, from (0, 42), left to right in catalog order, under a separate `parts` key of `atlas.json` (`rect` of
  the sheet and each face's rectangle in atlas texels). A model UV is the part's atlas offset plus the face's `uv`
  from the schema file (the rectangles alone do not give the polygon).
- **Models:** the player model and the NPC model both carry every polygon part (the NPC armor list is every catalog
  part; bandits wear cloth).
- **Zones:** as on the boxes, the painter's choice (cloth or leather in zone A, fittings in zone B); the zone rules
  of §6 count the hood with the head slot. The hood has no front-facing face, so it adds nothing to the zone count
  from the front.
- **Clearance:** clipping of the hood with the shoulders in some head poses is accepted (ruling 16); the offhand
  clearance check (back card, shields) leaves polygon parts out and the lane reports the hood's distance to the
  back card as a number.
- **Checks on the schema file** (`export.py --check` asserts them, so a new export from the page cannot break the
  model): faces planar and convex, winding outward, every hood edge shared by two hood faces or on the helm's
  surface, no face on the helm's surface, no two faces coplanar and overlapping, UVs inside `rect`, rectangles
  disjoint and inside `sheet`, the unwrap orientation above, symmetry about x = 0.
- `params` and `offsets` are the page's own controls (to reload a state on the page); the pipeline reads only
  `vertices`, `faces` and `sheet`.

## 2. Texture slots of a character

In this order in the model's texture list:

| Slot | Size | Content |
|---|---|---|
| `skin` | 64 × 32 | the race look plus the inner armor paint (§5), as before Round 54 |
| `cloak` | 32 × 32 | the cloak, as before |
| `armor` | 128 × 64 | the armor atlas (§3) |
| `weapon` | per weapon | the weapon card in the hand (inventory view; transparent in the world) |

An empty slot (no armor at all, no weapon shown) is `blank.png`, like the cloak today. The player model carries all
four slots. Round 55 appends the offhand, back and wings slots after them (NPCs: offhand and back;
[offhand-format.md](offhand-format.md) §5). Armored NPCs (a visual spec with an `armor_line`: guards and bandits; and, by the user's ruling of 2026-10-20,
kings, royal guards and generals in a royal variant of the guards' metal armor) use the generated **NPC model**:
the same body and armor buffer with `skin`, `armor`, `weapon` (no cloak), built from a list of the boxes and parts
NPCs wear; that list is **every catalog box and polygon part** for now (NPCs wear every tier). The royal variant colours zone A with the royal
cloth colour and zone B gold through the zone masks, so it needs no art of its own. Unarmored humanoid NPCs
(vendors, villagers) keep `character.b3d`.

The helmet is a box: when a head piece is worn, the race look drops the hair layer as before (the render tool
draws the bald look then), and the face stays the race's.

## 3. The armor atlas

`data/atlas.json` (written by `atlas.py`, checked by `atlas.py --check`) places every shape's box sheet once in a
**128 × 64** atlas: shelves left to right, sorted by sheet height, then width (descending), then id; no gutter; rows
0–41 used by the boxes. Per box: `rect` = [x, y, w, h] of its sheet and `faces` = its six face rectangles in atlas
texels, origin top left; a UV is u = x / 128, v = y / 64. The polygon parts follow under `parts` (§1a): the hood's
64 × 12 sheet at (0, 42), rows 42–53; rows 54–63 are free. The model's UVs, the baked layers and the game's
composition all use this one layout.

A baked atlas layer is the full 128 × 64 with only one slot's boxes and parts painted (§7); a character's armor
texture is its four slot layers overlaid, like the skin layers.

## 4. Box sheets

Each box has one sheet in the classic skin unwrap. Texel count per edge: `floor(size + 0.5)`, at least 1, from the
box's size in skin pixels (`w` = x, `h` = y, `d` = z; rotated boxes in their own axes). The sheet is `2d + 2w` wide
and `d + h` high, origin top left:

| Face | Position | Size |
|---|---|---|
| top | (d, 0) | w × d (its top row is the back edge) |
| bottom | (d + w, 0) | w × d (its top row is the front edge) |
| right (the character's right, −x) | (0, d) | d × h |
| front (+z) | (d, d) | w × h |
| left (+x) | (d + w, d) | d × h |
| back (−z) | (2d + w, d) | w × h |

Side faces: row 0 is the top. Front: column 0 is the character's right. Back: column 0 is the character's left.
Anything outside the six rectangles stays transparent. Faces are independent: transparency in a side face does not
remove the top or bottom face.

**Armor is opaque** (the user, 2026-10-20: transparency showed holes, since one looks into the hollow box): every
texel of every face of a box sheet is opaque. The only exception is the helm's **face window**: the texels of its
front face that lie wholly in front of the head's face (columns 1–8, rows 1–8 of the 10 × 10 front face) may stay
transparent, so a helmet can leave the face open; the head is directly behind them. Since Round 56 (ruling 17,
confirmed by the user 2026-10-22) the helm's **chin strip**, the front face's bottom row (all 10 texels), is
transparent on every cloth head piece (T1–T6) and on the leather ones without a veil (T1–T5); leather T6 and metal
keep it opaque. Since Round 57 (ruling 24) the strip continues **under the chin**: the helm's bottom face opens in the
rows that start in front of the neck (the body's front face, z = 2), full width, its rows 0–2 of 10 (the top row is
the front edge; `armorlib.chin_under`), on the same tiers; the head's underside is directly above them, and the rows
under the neck stay opaque. The hood reaches below the helm's top only behind the head, so nothing of it lies under
the chin. `check.py` allows both on a helm sheet that only those tiers wear and reports a zone shortfall of one
zone B pixel that the open strip causes on a head piece as a warning, not a violation (the shipped art has none since
the hood art, 0.56.1, repainted the leather T1 and T3 helms). Shape
comes from the box and the paint, not from cut-outs.

## 5. Inner paint (on the skin)

Per slot, a 64 × 32 image in the classic skin layout, painted only in the slot's own regions (fixed, so the draw
order of the slots does not matter):

| Slot | Regions (x0, y0)–(x1, y1), exclusive ends |
|---|---|
| chest | torso (16, 16)–(40, 32), arm (40, 16)–(56, 32) |
| legs | leg (0, 20)–(16, 28): the top of the leg to mid-calf |
| feet | leg (0, 28)–(16, 32): the lowest four rows |
| head | none (the helmet is a box; the face stays the race's) |

## 6. Painted art

The painter's files, in one art directory:

| File | What | Size |
|---|---|---|
| `<line>/<box>_<variant>.png` | a box sheet | §4 |
| `<line>/<part>_<variant>.png` | a polygon part's sheet (`hood`) | its schema's `sheet` (§1a) |
| `<line>/inner/<slot>_<variant>.png` | inner paint, slot chest, legs or feet | 64 × 32 |
| `<line>/icons/<slot>_<variant>.png` | inventory icon, slot head, chest, legs or feet | 16 × 16 |
| `tiers.json` | which boxes, variants and palette each tier uses | |
| `palettes.json` (optional) | proposed tier palettes, laid over `data/palettes.json` by name | |

`<line>` is `metal`, `leather` or `cloth`; `<variant>` a short id (`a`, `b`, …). One sheet per box or part, line
and variant serves every tier that names it; mirrored boxes have one sheet.

**Reference palette.** Paint only with these 13 colours plus full transparency; alpha is 0 or 255, nothing between.

| Index | Name | RGB | Meaning |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | M_HI | `#F0F0F0` | material highlight |
| 2 | M_LIGHT | `#C8C8C8` | material light |
| 3 | M_MID | `#A0A0A0` | material base |
| 4 | M_DARK | `#787878` | material shade |
| 5 | M_DEEP | `#505050` | material deep shade, seams |
| 6 | OUTLINE | `#282828` | gaps, slits, darkest line |
| 7 | ACC_LIGHT | `#A07850` | accent material (straps, under-leather, rope), light |
| 8 | ACC_DARK | `#6E5034` | accent material, dark |
| 9 | A_LIGHT | `#E07070` | zone A (prefix): cloth, tabard, lining, stole, light |
| 10 | A_DARK | `#B04848` | zone A, dark |
| 11 | B_LIGHT | `#70A0E0` | zone B (suffix): fittings, buckles, rivets, hem, gem, light |
| 12 | B_DARK | `#4870B0` | zone B, dark |
| 13 | GLOW | `#FFC040` | glow (ember seams); M_LIGHT on palettes without glow |

**Tier palettes** give each name a colour per tier (`data/palettes.json`, 18 palettes, all 13 names as `#RRGGBB`).
Zone A and B carry neutral colours there (undyed linen, bare metal or brass).

**Zones.** The prefix colours the cloth (zone A, indices 9–10), the suffix the fittings (zone B, 11–12). Every slot
of every tier shows, from the front, at least 6 zone A and 3 zone B pixels (box sheets and inner paint together;
only the body and the same slot's boxes count as hiding them), so one enchanted piece shows its colour.

**Icons.** 16 × 16, reference palette, palette-swapped per tier. An icon shows recognisably the same intended
piece, not a copy of the 3D shape: one clear silhouette, readable at 32 and 16 px, few details.

**`tiers.json`.** Every box may be used on every tier of every line; the painter chooses by the design goals. A
polygon part is named in `on` and `variant` like a box (the hood: cloth and leather only, always with the helm,
§1a).
A missing variant entry means `a`; a tier entry must name its `palette`, and one without `inner` or `icon` has none.

```json
{"metal": {"T1": {"palette": "bronze", "on": ["helm", "chest", "thigh", "boot"],
                  "variant": {"helm": "a", "chest": "a"}, "inner": {"chest": "a", "legs": "a", "feet": "a"},
                  "icon": {"head": "a", "chest": "a", "legs": "a", "feet": "a"}}}}
```

Tier `T<n>` of a line is the n-th grade of that line in `grug_gear.MATERIALS` (metal bronze … abyssal_steel,
leather light … nightscale, cloth patch … stormweave).

## 7. Baked game files

Written by the bake from the art, the palettes, `tiers.json` and the atlas; read by `grug_visuals`. `<grade>` is
the game's grade key (`grug_gear.MATERIALS`; leather T4 keeps the key `scaled` under the display name Hardened).
The atlas and skin layers and their masks go to `mods/PLAYER/grug_visuals/textures/`, the icons stay in
`mods/ITEMS/grug_gear/textures/`. Every line, grade and slot gets its files, empty (transparent) where the tier
paints nothing: 72 atlas layers (head, chest, legs, feet) and 54 skin layers (chest, legs, feet; the head has no
skin region), each with its mask.

| File | Content |
|---|---|
| `grug_armor_<line>_<grade>_<slot>.png` | atlas layer, 128 × 64, only this slot's boxes and parts painted |
| `grug_armor_<line>_<grade>_<slot>_skin.png` | skin layer, 64 × 32, the slot's inner paint |
| `…_ench.png` next to each | the enchant mask in today's format (grey + alpha, two frames stacked: frame 0 zone A for the prefix colour, frame 1 zone B for the suffix colour, grey from the art's shading), derived from the zone pixels, never drawn separately |
| `grug_gear_item_<slot>_<line>_<grade>.png` | the item icon (+ its mask), names unchanged |

## 8. Checks

From `tools/r54_armor/`:

- `python3 check.py --art <dir>`: file names and sizes, colours, alpha, transparent texels in a face (outside the
  helm's face window and, on the chin-strip tiers, its chin strip and the strip under the chin; a polygon part's
  whole face rectangles),
  paint outside faces or regions, a part worn without its helm, missing files per tier, zone counts from the
  front, the overlap report (boxes only); exit 1 on a violation.
- `python3 render.py --art <dir> --out <dir>`: contact sheets per line and race (`--detail` for one tier,
  `--engine soft` by default, sandbox-safe).
- `python3 export.py --check`, `python3 atlas.py --check`, `python3 zfight.py`: the contract data is current, the
  polygon parts' schema files pass the §1a invariants (`polypart.py`) and the geometry is clean.
- `test_art/` passes `check.py` without warnings; `test_art_broken/` exercises the violations.
